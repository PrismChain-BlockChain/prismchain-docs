# 1. PURPOSE

This experiment investigates whether the proposed Spectral Mathematics framework can reveal useful structure in geometric, topological, and cohomological objects associated with the **Hodge Conjecture**.

The objective is not to assume that the Hodge Conjecture is true, nor to claim that spectral coordinates provide a proof.

The objective is to construct controlled mathematical representations, map them into spectral space, measure their relationships, search for stable structure, and determine whether those observations can be translated back into conventional mathematics.

The central question is:

> **Does geometric and cohomological structure become measurably organized when represented in spectral space?**

A successful experiment would not be established by an attractive visualization.

It would require:

1. reproducible spectral representations,
2. recovery of known mathematical structure,
3. robustness under representation changes,
4. separation from randomized controls,
5. blind predictive utility,
6. and eventually a mathematically rigorous translation of any surviving relationship.

---

# 2. TARGET MATHEMATICAL PROBLEM

The Hodge Conjecture concerns the relationship between:

* algebraic cycles,
* cohomology classes,
* Hodge decomposition,
* and rational Hodge classes

on smooth projective complex varieties.

Very roughly, the conjecture proposes that certain rational cohomology classes of Hodge type `(p,p)` arise from algebraic cycles.

For a smooth projective complex variety \(X\), the complex cohomology has a Hodge decomposition:

$$
H^k(X,\mathbb{C})
=
\bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(X)
$$

A Hodge class in degree \(2p\) is a rational cohomology class whose complexification lies in:

$$
H^{p,p}(X)
$$

The Hodge Conjecture asks whether such rational Hodge classes are generated, in the appropriate rational sense, by classes associated with algebraic cycles of codimension \(p\).

This experiment does **not** treat that statement as established.

Instead, it asks whether the relationships among:

```text
GEOMETRY
   ↓
TOPOLOGY
   ↓
COHOMOLOGY
   ↓
HODGE DECOMPOSITION
   ↓
HODGE CLASSES
   ↓
ALGEBRAIC CYCLES
```

produce measurable organization in spectral space.

# 34. FALSIFICATION PROGRAM

The experiment should actively search for cases where the proposed spectral relationship fails.

Failure conditions include:

* equivalent objects map inconsistently,
* known Hodge structures cannot be recovered,
* spectral patterns disappear under valid basis changes,
* relationships disappear under small numerical changes,
* randomized controls reproduce the same patterns,
* holdout prediction fails,
* candidate invariants fail transformation tests,
* inversion produces uncontrolled false positives,
* or conventional mathematics cannot interpret the result.

A failed hypothesis is a legitimate experimental result.
