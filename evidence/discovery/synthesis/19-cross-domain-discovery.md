---

# 1. Purpose

This experiment investigates whether a measurable mathematical relationship exists between the proposed spectral coordinate system and fractal or recursively generated mathematical structures.

The central idea is simple:

> **Light produces structured spectral relationships. Fractal systems produce structured recursive relationships. Does a mathematical correspondence exist between the two?**

This experiment does **not** assume that such a relationship exists.

It does not assume that fractals are “made of light,” that spectral coordinates are inherently fractal, or that one mathematical system can be reduced to the other.

Instead, the experiment asks whether independently generated spectral and fractal structures exhibit reproducible relationships that can be measured, modeled, and independently verified.

---

# 2. Research Objective

The primary objective is to determine whether the proposed Spectral Mathematics framework can identify structural relationships between:

```text
SPECTRAL STRUCTURE
        ↕
GEOMETRIC STRUCTURE
        ↕
RECURSIVE STRUCTURE
        ↕
FRACTAL STRUCTURE
```

The investigation should determine:

1. whether fractal objects can be represented in spectral coordinates,
2. whether recursive transformations produce stable spectral signatures,
3. whether different fractal families occupy distinguishable regions of spectral space,
4. whether mathematically equivalent fractal representations produce related spectral structures,
5. whether recursion depth produces measurable spectral organization,
6. whether spectral resonance corresponds to known fractal relationships,
7. whether spectral inversion can recover candidate recursive structures,
8. whether any observed relationship survives controls and independent representations.

# 3. Core Hypothesis

### Hypothesis

> **Fractal structures may contain measurable spectral organization that can be represented within the proposed spectral coordinate system.**

This is a research hypothesis.

It is not an established mathematical law.

A successful experiment would provide evidence for a relationship.

It would not establish that spectral mathematics and fractal mathematics are fundamentally identical.

# 4. Competing Hypotheses

The experiment should test multiple possibilities.

### H₀ — No meaningful relationship

Spectral representations of fractal structures do not reveal relationships beyond what conventional geometric or statistical analysis already explains.

### H₁ — Representational relationship

Fractal structures produce reproducible spectral signatures.

### H₂ — Recursive spectral relationship

Recursive generation produces systematic changes in spectral coordinates.

### H₃ — Cross-family relationship

Different fractal families share higher-level spectral structures despite having different construction rules.

### H₄ — Spectral–fractal correspondence

A mathematically definable relationship exists between recursive transformations and spectral transformations.

### H₅ — Transformational equivalence

Certain classes of fractal transformations correspond to identifiable transformations in spectral space.

Each hypothesis must remain falsifiable.

$$
\{f_1,f_2,\ldots,f_n\}
$$

Repeated application produces:

$$

=
