# 📄 PrismChain Research — Papers

> **Papers preserve the intellectual history of the ecosystem.**

The Papers library is the public index of research papers, white papers, technical essays, architectural documents, and other formal research publications associated with the PrismChain ecosystem.

Its purpose is to preserve the evolution of ideas while making an important distinction:

> **A paper can document an idea without defining the current architecture.**

The PrismChain ecosystem has evolved through research, experimentation, implementation, and architectural refinement.

Older papers therefore remain valuable as historical and intellectual lineage even when terminology, architecture, or research conclusions have changed.

A paper represents the state of understanding at the time it was written.

* working hypotheses;

* ideas that were abandoned;

Therefore, papers should be read within their historical context.

> **Research evolves. The archive preserves that evolution.**

---

# 3. Current vs. Historical Material

The public library distinguishes between current and historical documents.

### Current

Documents that remain aligned with the present public architecture.

### Historical

Documents that preserve earlier thinking but may contain concepts that have since changed.

### Superseded

Documents whose architecture or terminology has been replaced by a later understanding.

### Research

Documents describing active questions or hypotheses.

### Experimental

Documents describing experiments or prototypes.

### Private

Research that exists but is intentionally not published.

# 15. Papers and Current Specifications

A paper and a specification serve different purposes.

A paper may explain:

* why an idea exists;
* what problem it addresses;
* what was believed at the time;
* what research supported it.

A current specification may define:

* what the architecture currently requires;
* what interfaces exist;
* what boundaries are recognized;
* what behavior is currently expected.

Therefore:

> **Do not use an old paper as a substitute for the current architecture.**

Likewise:

> **Do not erase an old paper simply because the architecture changed.**

The history matters.

# 17. Paper Review Questions

When evaluating a paper against the current architecture, ask:

### What was the paper trying to explain?

Identify the original problem.

### What assumptions did it make?

Separate assumptions from established results.

### What was later tested?

Identify experiments and implementation evidence.

### What changed?

Document architectural evolution.

### What remains valid?

Preserve useful conclusions.

### What was superseded?

Identify concepts that no longer represent the current system.

### What remains unresolved?

Keep open questions visible.

---

# 18. Evidence and Publication

A paper should not be treated as evidence merely because it exists.

Publication establishes that an idea was documented.

Evidence establishes whether a claim is supported.

The distinction is:

```text id="w5q8cn"
PAPER
  ↓
CLAIM
  ↓
TEST
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
CONCLUSION
```

A well-written paper can explain a hypothesis.

It cannot make an untested hypothesis true.

---

# 19. Research Integrity

The Papers library should preserve intellectual honesty.

It should not:

* hide failed research;
* present hypotheses as facts;
* present historical architecture as current architecture;
* imply implementation where none exists;
* imply proof where only analogy exists;
* imply production capability from a prototype;
* or use scientific terminology without defining its meaning.

The goal is not to make every paper appear successful.

The goal is to preserve the actual research journey.

# 20. Public and Private Publications

Not every research document belongs in the public archive.

Private material may include:

* unpublished mathematics;
* proprietary algorithms;
* internal prompts;
* private AI architectures;
* confidential datasets;
* unreleased experiments;
* security-sensitive research;
* proprietary implementation details.

A public paper may describe the research question and its significance without publishing the mechanism that provides competitive advantage.

> **Reveal the research. Protect the mechanism.**

# 25. What the Papers Library Does Not Do

The Papers library does not:

* replace the current architecture documentation;
* declare every historical idea current;
* expose proprietary research;
* turn hypotheses into facts;
* substitute publication for evidence;
* guarantee that every proposed system will be built;
* or prevent the architecture from changing.

Its job is to preserve the record.

# 26. The Historical Principle

An evolving technical ecosystem should not be embarrassed by its earlier ideas.

Earlier research demonstrates how questions developed.

Later research demonstrates what was learned.

The correct response to architectural evolution is not to erase history.

It is to contextualize it.

> **Preserve the lineage. Clarify the evolution. Identify the current truth.**
