# Spectral Forge — Experiment 02: Inverse Discovery

**Status:** 🔵 Research
**Experiment:** 02
**System:** Spectral Forge
**Research Program:** Spectral Forge Experimental Evidence Program
**Classification:** Inverse Discovery
**Implementation Status:** Not yet experimentally demonstrated

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this experiment is to determine whether Spectral Forge can begin with an existing structure and discover a corresponding representation in terms of Spectral Mathematics.

The experiment reverses the direction tested in Experiment 01.

Experiment 01 investigates:

```text
OBJECTIVE
+
CONSTRAINTS
        ↓
SPECTRAL MATHEMATICS
        ↓
SPECTRAL FORGE
        ↓
STRUCTURE
```

Experiment 02 investigates:

```text
EXISTING STRUCTURE
        ↓
SPECTRAL FORGE
        ↓
SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
MATHEMATICAL RELATIONSHIP
```

The central question is whether the Forge can recover meaningful mathematical structure from an observed structure without being given the answer in advance.

This experiment therefore begins the investigation of **inverse discovery**.

---

# 2. Central Question

> **Can Spectral Forge infer a valid and meaningful Spectral Mathematics representation from an existing structure without being explicitly given that representation?**

A successful result must establish more than the ability to assign arbitrary spectral labels to an existing object.

The discovered representation must have measurable explanatory or reconstructive value.

The experiment therefore asks whether:

```text
STRUCTURE
        ↓
DISCOVERY
        ↓
SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
RELATIONSHIP
```

produces a representation that can independently account for properties of the original structure.

---

# 3. Scientific Position

The working hypothesis is:

> **If an existing structure contains relationships that can be expressed through Spectral Mathematics, Spectral Forge may be able to recover a corresponding spectral representation from the structure itself.**

This is a hypothesis.

The experiment must not assume that every structure possesses a spectral representation.

It must also not assume that a representation discovered by the Forge is meaningful merely because it can be made to fit the observed structure.

The research distinction is therefore:

```text
FITTING
≠
DISCOVERY
```

A representation that merely describes every observed feature after extensive adjustment may have little predictive or explanatory value.

The experiment must therefore test whether the discovered representation can independently reproduce or predict structural properties.

---

The system under test is:

Spectral Forge remains separate from:

The system boundary is:

# 5. Hypothesis

## Primary Hypothesis

> **Spectral Forge can infer a spectral representation from an existing structure such that the representation captures measurable relationships within that structure and can independently reproduce or predict defined structural properties.**

## Secondary Hypotheses

### H1 — Structural Recovery

The Forge can identify relationships present in the input structure.

### H2 — Spectral Representation

Those relationships can be expressed through the available Spectral Mathematics representation.

### H3 — Reconstruction

The discovered representation can reconstruct relevant properties of the original structure.

### H4 — Predictive Value

The representation can predict withheld or previously unexposed structural properties.

### H5 — Stability

Equivalent observations of the same underlying structure should produce equivalent or mathematically equivalent representations.

### H6 — Non-Memorization

The discovered representation should not simply be a stored description of the original input.

---

# 6. Definitions and Distinctions

## 6.1 Inverse Discovery

For this experiment:

> **Inverse discovery is the process of beginning with an existing structure and inferring a mathematical representation capable of describing or reconstructing relevant properties of that structure.**

The direction is:

```text
STRUCTURE
    ↓
MATHEMATICAL RELATIONSHIP
```

---

## 6.2 Observed Structure

The observed structure is the input presented to the Forge.

It must exist independently of the Forge's discovery process.

The experiment should record how the structure was produced or obtained.

---

## 6.3 Hidden Representation

Where possible, the test structure should have a known underlying representation that is withheld from the Forge.

This creates a controlled environment in which discovery can be evaluated.

The hidden representation acts as an experimental reference, not as information available to the Forge.

---

## 6.4 Discovered Representation

The discovered representation is the output produced by Spectral Forge.

It must be recorded exactly as generated.

It must not be manually corrected before evaluation.

---

## 6.5 Mathematical Equivalence

Different mathematical parameterizations may represent the same underlying structure.

Therefore, exact parameter equality should not always be required.

The experiment must define an equivalence relation where appropriate.

For example:

```text
representation A
        ≈
representation B
```

may be considered equivalent if both generate the same relevant structural relationships.

This prevents false failures caused by different but mathematically equivalent representations.

---

## 6.6 Reconstruction

Reconstruction means using the discovered representation to recreate the relevant properties of the original structure.

Reconstruction must be performed independently from the discovery process.

---

# 7. Test Design

The initial experiment should use a structure whose mathematical properties can be independently characterized.

The preferred progression is:

```text
KNOWN STRUCTURE
        ↓
HIDDEN GENERATING CONDITIONS
        ↓
OBSERVED BY FORGE
        ↓
INVERSE DISCOVERY
        ↓
DISCOVERED SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
RECONSTRUCTION
        ↓
COMPARISON WITH ORIGINAL
```

The hidden generating conditions should not be supplied directly to the Forge.

The purpose is to determine whether they can be recovered from the observable structure.

---

# 8. Experimental Procedure

## Step 1 — Construct or Select the Test Structure

Create or select a structure with:

* a known mathematical origin,
* measurable properties,
* reproducible generation,
* and an independently verifiable representation.

The test structure should be simple enough that the expected relationships are understood.

---

## Step 2 — Hide the Generating Representation

Separate the reference representation from the observable structure.

The Forge receives only the permitted observation.

The reference representation remains available only to the evaluator.

---

## Step 3 — Define the Observation Boundary

Document exactly what the Forge is allowed to observe.

For example:

```text
OBSERVABLE
- structural coordinates
- relationships
- topology
- measured properties

HIDDEN
- original spectral parameters
- generating equations
- target representation
```

The actual boundary should match the experiment.

---

## Step 4 — Run Inverse Discovery

Provide the observed structure to Spectral Forge.

Allow the Forge to produce its candidate spectral representation.

Record the complete output.

Do not manually adjust the result before evaluation.

---

## Step 5 — Evaluate the Discovered Representation

Determine whether the representation:

1. describes the observed structure,
2. satisfies predefined mathematical conditions,
3. reproduces relevant structural properties,
4. remains valid under independent evaluation,
5. and agrees with the known reference where equivalence can be established.

---

## Step 6 — Reconstruction

Use only the discovered representation to reconstruct the relevant structure.

The reconstruction procedure must not use the original hidden representation.

Compare:

```text
ORIGINAL STRUCTURE
        ↕
RECONSTRUCTED STRUCTURE
```

---

## Step 7 — Holdout Evaluation

Where possible, divide the structure into observed and withheld properties.

The Forge receives enough information to discover the representation but does not receive every property that will later be evaluated.

The discovered representation is then tested against the withheld properties.

This is critical.

A representation that explains only what it was explicitly shown may simply be fitting.

A representation that successfully predicts withheld structure provides stronger evidence of discovery.

---

# 9. Controls

## Control A — Direct Parameter Fitting

Use a conventional fitting procedure against the same structure.

This establishes how well a non-spectral baseline can reproduce the input.

---

## Control B — Arbitrary Representation

Attempt to construct an unrestricted representation capable of fitting the observed structure.

This demonstrates how easily a sufficiently flexible system can create an apparently successful explanation.

---

## Control C — Random Representation

Generate random candidate representations and evaluate their reconstruction performance.

This establishes a baseline for accidental correspondence.

---

## Control D — Partial Observation

Remove selected structural information from the Forge's input.

Determine whether the discovered representation can recover the withheld information.

---

## Control E — Perturbed Structure

Introduce controlled changes to the observed structure.

Determine whether the discovered representation responds coherently.

---

# 10. Test Classes

## Test Class A — Basic Inverse Recovery

> Can the Forge recover a known underlying representation from an observable structure?

---

## Test Class B — Reconstruction

Question:

---

## Test Class C — Parameter Recovery

Question:

---

## Test Class D — Withheld Property Prediction

Question:

---

Question:

> Can the Forge identify when multiple mathematically equivalent representations describe the same structure?

---

Question:

> Does a controlled change in the structure produce a corresponding change in the discovered representation?

---

## Test Class G — Unseen Structure

Question:

> Can the Forge discover representations for structures that were not included in any predefined template or example set?

# 11. Measurements

The following measurements should be recorded where applicable.

## Representation Error

Difference between the discovered representation and the known reference representation under the predefined equivalence rule.

---

## Reconstruction Error

Difference between the original structure and the structure reconstructed from the discovered representation.

---

## Constraint Satisfaction

Number or percentage of known mathematical constraints recovered by the representation.

---

## Predictive Accuracy

Accuracy on withheld structural properties.

---

## Stability

Variation between representations discovered from equivalent observations.

---

## Complexity

Measure the complexity of the discovered representation.

This is important because an excessively complicated representation may simply encode the observed structure rather than reveal a useful underlying relationship.

Where appropriate:

```text
simpler valid representation
        >
unnecessarily complex fitted representation
```

The exact complexity metric must be defined by the implementation.

---

## Baseline Performance

Compare Forge performance against the control methods.

---

# 12. Negative Controls

Inverse discovery requires strong negative controls.

The Forge should receive structures for which:

* no valid representation exists within the tested mathematical model,
* constraints are contradictory,
* observations are deliberately corrupted,
* or the structure lies outside the declared representational domain.

The expected result must be defined beforehand.

A system that produces a confident spectral explanation for every possible input may be overfitting, unconstrained, or incapable of recognizing representational failure.

Failure to find a representation can therefore be an important positive scientific result.

A complete evidence package should contain:

4. The information withheld from the Forge.
5. The discovered representation.

7. Independent comparison between original and reconstructed structure.

9. Control results.

13. Raw data and metadata.

The evidence must allow an independent reviewer to determine whether the Forge actually discovered a relationship or merely produced a flexible description.

---

# 15. Acceptance Criteria

Experiment 02 should be considered supported only if:

1. An independently defined structure is provided to the Forge.
2. The underlying reference representation is hidden during discovery.
3. The Forge produces a candidate spectral representation.
4. The representation satisfies predefined mathematical validity conditions.
5. The representation reconstructs relevant properties of the original structure.
6. The reconstruction is independently verified.
7. The representation demonstrates predictive or explanatory value beyond simply reproducing supplied observations.
8. Results are reproducible.
9. Control comparisons are completed.
10. Negative controls behave according to their predefined expectations.
11. No hidden target-specific information is required.

A successful reconstruction alone is insufficient if the system simply memorizes the input or constructs an unconstrained lookup representation.

---

# 16. Failure Conditions

The experiment fails or remains inconclusive if:

* no valid representation can be discovered;
* the representation only reproduces the observed input without predictive value;
* the discovered representation requires hidden reference information;
* reconstruction depends on the original structure rather than the discovered representation;
* the representation is arbitrarily adjusted after discovery;
* the system produces valid-looking representations for impossible inputs without detecting the failure;
* results cannot be reproduced;
* or the spectral representation cannot be distinguished from a generic fitted model.

Failure does not invalidate Spectral Forge as a whole.

It identifies the limits of the tested inverse-discovery mechanism.

# 17. Implementation vs Specification

The conceptual architecture currently proposes:

```text
EXISTING STRUCTURE
        ↓
SPECTRAL FORGE
        ↓
SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
MATHEMATICAL RELATIONSHIP
```

This is a research model.

It is not evidence that the current implementation performs inverse discovery.

The actual implementation may reveal that inverse discovery requires:

* preprocessing,
* decomposition,
* recursive analysis,
* optimization,
* constraint extraction,
* symbolic transformation,
* multiple candidate representations,
* or another mechanism.

The experiment must document what the implementation actually does.

The specification should be updated afterward if the implementation reveals a more accurate architecture.

# 21. Limitations

## Representation Ambiguity

Multiple mathematical representations may describe the same structure.

Therefore, exact parameter recovery may be impossible or scientifically unnecessary.

---

## Observation Limits

The Forge can only discover relationships present in the information it is allowed to observe.

An incomplete observation may make the inverse problem underdetermined.

---

## Non-Uniqueness

Different underlying mechanisms may generate the same observable structure.

Successful reconstruction therefore does not automatically identify the original generating mechanism.

---

## Overfitting

A sufficiently flexible system may create representations that explain observed data without revealing a useful underlying relationship.

---

## Complexity

A discovered representation that is technically valid but excessively complex may have limited scientific value.

---

## Domain Dependence

A successful experiment on one class of structures does not establish general inverse-discovery capability.

# 24. Final Principle

Experiment 02 asks the inverse question to Experiment 01:

> **If Spectral Mathematics can be used to construct a structure, can the structure itself reveal a corresponding spectral representation?**

The scientific target is not merely:

```text
STRUCTURE
↓
A LABEL
```

It is:

```text
STRUCTURE
        ↓
SPECTRAL REPRESENTATION
        ↓
MATHEMATICAL RELATIONSHIP
        ↓
RECONSTRUCTION / PREDICTION
```

The distinction is critical.

> **A representation that merely fits a structure is not automatically a discovered mathematical relationship.**

The experiment therefore requires independent validation, controls, withheld information, reproducibility, and explicit separation of discovery from memorization.

The objective is not to force the Forge to find a spectral explanation.

The objective is to determine whether one can actually be discovered.

> **Let the structure reveal the mathematics—or reveal that the proposed relationship is not present.**
