# 🌈 Light Lab

> **Study the structure of light. Discover what mathematics it reveals.**

The Light Lab is the public research area for investigating light, spectral structure, and the mathematical relationships that may emerge from them.

It is not a claim that PrismChain is physically made of light.

It is not a replacement for established physics.

It is a research environment for asking whether properties and structures associated with light can reveal useful mathematical relationships that can be expressed computationally.

The guiding principle is simple:

> **Understand the phenomenon before abstracting it.**

---

# 1. Purpose

The Light Lab exists to investigate the mathematical and structural properties associated with light and spectral phenomena.

Research may examine:

* wavelength;
* frequency;
* phase;
* amplitude;
* color;
* spectral separation;
* interference;
* resonance;
* coherence;
* geometry;
* periodicity;
* relationships between spectral components;
* coordinate systems;
* information representation.

The purpose is not to force these properties into software.

The purpose is to determine which relationships are mathematically meaningful and whether any of them can support useful computational structures.

---

# 2. The Research Direction

The broad research progression is:

```text id="7q4mnc"
PHYSICAL PHENOMENON
        ↓
OBSERVATION
        ↓
MATHEMATICAL DESCRIPTION
        ↓
STRUCTURAL RELATIONSHIP
        ↓
COMPUTATIONAL MODEL
        ↓
EXPERIMENT
        ↓

```

This distinction is important.

A physical observation is not automatically a mathematical proof.

A mathematical relationship is not automatically a computational advantage.

A computational analogy is not automatically a valid model of the underlying physical system.

Each step requires its own justification.

The visible spectrum is commonly associated with colors such as:

# 6. Wavelength

Wavelength provides one way of describing electromagnetic radiation.

Research may therefore examine whether spectral coordinates can provide useful computational representations.

Potential questions include:

* What information is preserved?
* What information is discarded?
* Which relationships remain invariant?

# 7. Frequency

Frequency provides another description of oscillatory phenomena.

The relationship between wavelength and frequency demonstrates that apparently different descriptions can represent the same underlying phenomenon under appropriate conditions.

This is useful as a research example because it raises a broader computational question:

> **Can the same underlying structure be represented through multiple mathematically equivalent coordinate systems?**

This question may become important when designing spectral representations.

# 8. Amplitude

Amplitude describes the magnitude of an oscillatory quantity.

Any such equivalence must be demonstrated.

     ↓

     ↓

     ↓

     ↓

* the method;
